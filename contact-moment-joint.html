Timber contact moment joint

Feasibility prototype — not for design use. The calculation has not been validated for design decisions.

A timber-to-timber interface resists compression by contact and tension by screws. The page finds the largest moment capacity at the entered axial force, with a full neutral-axis scan and tolerance checked local refinement.

Checked

  • Contact compression at an angle to grain, EN 1995-1-1 6.2.3(2)
  • Axial screw withdrawal, head pull-through and tension, EN 1995-1-1 8.7.2
  • Equilibrium and moment capacity about one axis; numerical search reports its stopping reason

Not checked

  • Interface shear/friction, lateral screw action and axial/lateral interaction
  • Screw spacing, splitting, member checks away from the interface and other bending axes
  • Contact stiffness and ductility are assumptions in the indicative FE check below

Indicative FE: assumed contact stiffness (L_c) and ductility (mu_c); EN 1995 gives no values for these. The EN check decides.

Plane sections with compression-only timber springs and tension-only screws check whether the plastic compression block can develop before a screw reaches capacity. Withdrawal is brittle by default.
