Validation and scope

Feasibility prototype — not for design use. The comparisons below show how far the tools have been checked so far. They do not make the tools a validated design aid.

This page lists what each connection tool has been compared with, the result of each comparison and what has not been compared. The pages themselves state what each check covers.

These comparisons run as automated tests with every change of the tools. Apart from the T-stub, none of the checks has been compared with physical tests. The finite-element checks run at γ = 1 and are a cross-check of the EN model, not a design resistance.

What the comparison shows

  • T2 (mode 2): the FE and the EN resistance agree within 1 % and both lie 4–5 % above the test maximum. The FE is stiffer than the test: 8–14 % more force at the same separation. Both are compared at γ = 1 with measured strengths; the partial factors of a design check are not part of this comparison.
  • T1 (mode 1, thin flange): the test load keeps rising to 203 kN at a separation of 9.7 mm, through strain hardening and membrane action at large deformation. Neither the EN model (134 kN) nor the FE resistance (the load at 5 % plastic strain, with small-displacement kinematics) counts on that reserve. Up to a separation of 2 mm the FE is 3–6 % below the test.
  • Distance m: the shell model has no root fillet. With m measured from the web face it is 19–47 % below the tests; with the EN m (0.8 r off the web face) it comes within 0–9 % of the EN resistance (T1 lies between EN methods 1 and 2). The T-stub page therefore asks for the EN m for rolled sections.

Status of T1

In the first comparison (11 October 2026) T1 with the EN geometry stopped with a singular stiffness matrix: with an odd number of flange elements across the width the web was not connected to the flange. The mesh now always has an even number of elements across the flange, and an automated test checks the connection for the T1 and T2 widths and solves T1 with the exact EN m. Rerun on 20 October 2026: T1 146.3 kN and T2 205.3 kN, without solver failure.

Limits of this comparison

  • Two specimens, one bolt size (M12 8.8), one length and one bolt layout; no preloaded bolts.
  • Not compared: bolt bending, flange and web of different strength (T1's web is 9 % stronger than its flange), several bolt rows, welded T-stubs, end plates and column flanges.
  • The FE resistance is an event (5 % plastic strain or bolt yield), not the ultimate load; the model has small-displacement kinematics and no fracture.
  • The test values come from the figures of an author preprint, not from the original test records.

Limits of all tools

  • Feasibility prototype: the tools have not been reviewed for design use.
  • Each page states what it checks and what it does not; the connected members, the foundation and the overall structure are never checked.
  • Partial factors default to the recommended Eurocode values; check the National Annex that applies to the project.
  • Inputs are not checked against product data (sections, anchors, masonry units).
