Pinned column base with cast-in anchors

Feasibility prototype — not for design use. The calculation has not been validated for design decisions.

A steel column (I/H or hollow section) on a base plate, grout and a concrete foundation, with four headed anchor bolts in two rows beyond the column. Two load cases: compression with shear, and uplift with shear. The check covers the concrete in compression, the base plate and anchors in tension, shear by friction and anchors, the anchorage in concrete and the column weld; a finite-element model of the base plate checks the uplift resistance of the component model. Everything runs in this browser.

Checked

  • Concrete in compression under the plate (EN 1993-1-8 6.2.5, 6.2.8.2; α from EN 1992-1-1 6.7)
  • Uplift: base plate and anchors (6.2.6.11, Table 6.2); shear by friction and anchors (6.2.2)
  • Anchorage per EN 1992-4 (steel, pull-out, cone, pry-out, concrete edge), column weld, detailing
  • FE shell model of plate, column stub, anchors and contact, as a cross-check of the uplift resistance at γ = 1 (compression: indicative)

Not checked

  • Fixed bases (moment at the base), post-installed anchors, blow-out, anchor reinforcement
  • The column, the foundation and its reinforcement
  • In the FE model: the anchorage in concrete, the welds, anchor bending and shear

FE verification (in this browser)

Shell model of the base plate and the column stub with plasticity, anchors as tension springs (elastic length Lb, yield at 0.9 fub As) and contact on a rigid support, loaded step by step to 5 % plastic strain or anchor yield. It uses characteristic strengths (γ = 1) and is compared with the EN component model at γ = 1 — a check of the component model, not a design value. The design actions above are not used. Where the 5 % strain is reached at the corner of the column flange or wall weld, the FE value depends on the mesh (lower with a finer mesh); the report says so.
